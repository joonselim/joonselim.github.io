GraspGrade

Hackathon proposal

October 21, 2026, Inseok Lee, Joonse Lim

In one line

GraspGrade takes first-person videos of hands using objects, cuts them into clips at the moment of contact, and gives each clip a score for how likely it is that a robot hand can copy it.

We check two things with real ground truth, not with our own guesses. The contact cut is checked against a touch-sensing glove. The robot score is checked against motion capture data from Meta HOT3D.

Theme: Push the Frontier (robotics).

The problem

Robot hand teams want human video because there is so much of it and it is cheap. But most of it is not usable for teaching a robot hand.

Fingers get hidden, the camera shakes, and from video alone it is hard to tell when the hand really touches the object.

Papers that clean internet hand video report that only a small share of clips survive their filters. Each team writes its own filter, and they usually list the reasons for dropping a clip but do not give a score that others can reuse.

Today the simple option is to ask a vision model “is this clip usable?” with no fixed rules. We think a clear checklist, checked against real sensor data, can do better. We want to measure if that is true.

What we will build

Three parts, all running on video only.

  1. Cut at contact. SAM 3.1 tracks the hand and the objects. Their overlap gives a contact signal, and Muse Spark picks the exact first and last touch frame. Each touch becomes one clip.
  2. Score each clip. Seven checks, such as hidden fingers, camera shake, and whether the hand shape fits a robot hand, combined into a 0 to 100 score.
  3. A simple viewer. A web page with clips sorted by score, a contact timeline, and a score card for each clip.

What we will measure

Two numbers. Each one has its own ground truth, so one answers “when” and the other answers “can a robot do it”.

How we label a clip as “copyable” in HOT3D. We take the motion capture hand, map it to the Allegro hand with dex-retargeting, and put the Allegro wrist where the human wrist is. In the contact frames, the clip counts as copyable if the thumb and at least one other robot fingertip are close to the object surface and no fingertip goes deep into the object. We fix the distance limits on the tuning split before we look at the test split.

All tuning uses a separate split, and every number we report comes from held-out data.

10-day plan

The build is mostly glue between tools that already exist (SAM 3.1, Muse Spark, MediaPipe, dex-retargeting, the HOT3D toolkit). The hard part is the measurement, so most days go there.

  1. Day 1 to 2Download OpenGraph and a HOT3D subset, fix the splits, connect SAM 3.1 and Muse Spark, measure cost and speed on one episode.
  2. Day 3 to 4Contact cutting on OpenGraph. Measure the time gap between “looks touched” and “glove feels pressure” and correct for it. Number 1.
  3. Day 5 to 6HOT3D labels. Undistort the fisheye frames, run motion capture hands through dex-retargeting, check fingertips against object models.
  4. Day 7The seven checks and the baseline on HOT3D clips. Learn weights. Number 2.
  5. Day 8 to 9Viewer, demo video, README.
  6. Day 10Final check and submit before noon ET.

Limits we know about

Our robot check is geometric, not a physics test. We test one robot hand, the Allegro. We test on lab data first.
